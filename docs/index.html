Would this mortgage file pass a first review?

Banking audit case · Fictional buyer

A bank needs a defensible loan decision. This practice review checks the calculations and separates a modeled result from evidence that still needs to be collected.

The audit always reviews the selected buyer’s baseline. It follows public OSFI B-20 documentation and review principles. The 39%/44% debt-service screen is a CMHC-style comparison; an uninsured lender’s limits may differ. This is an independent portfolio exercise, not TD policy, a compliance opinion or a loan approval.

    Why did the income hurdle rise?

    2020–2023 observed inputs

    The line compares income needed at the minimum down payment with the median income of families who actually bought. It provides market context, not a test of whether a typical renter qualifies.

    Price, rates or income?

    2021–2023 contribution to the change in modeled required income minus observed buyer-family median income. All six input-replacement orders are averaged. Descriptive accounting, not causal attribution.

    What about the later rate cuts?

    The observed 2025 national rate averages are applied to the same 2023 price and unchanged profile. This isolates rates; it does not measure observed 2025 affordability.

    What do first-time-buyer measures actually change?

    Cash, payments and trade-offs

    Eligibility is assumed, not verified. HBP withdrawals move existing RRSP assets; they are not new wealth. The FHSA illustration counts contributions once and adds only a refund at an assumed 30% marginal rate. The refund timing is also assumed.

    Does waiting to save put the home closer?

    A sensitivity test, not a forecast

    More saving reduces the loan. Rising prices or rent can offset the gain. The three lines show what happens under falling, flat and rising prices.

    Income did not keep up with consumption.

    Canada · Renter averages · 2020 to 2025

    In 2025, annual income was CAD 70,099 and consumption was CAD 73,669 per renter household. The gap was −CAD 3,570. These are national account averages, not a household's bank statement.

    More detail: saving reconciliation and the earlier cash illustration

    How reported saving adds up

    Positive saving needs a closer look.

    In the final quarter of 2025, renters showed positive net saving of CAD 269. But that figure included a pension adjustment.

    Income was below consumption. The adjustment made reported saving positive; it did not establish cash available for a deposit.

    Adding the four actual quarters gives −CAD 557 of reported net saving for the full year.

    What the audit changes

    Check the evidence before calling someone ready to buy.

    Housing need shows the pressure renters face. The saving reconciliation and cash scenarios show why a positive saving headline cannot settle the buying question. Verify available funds, the full purchase budget and mortgage eligibility before making that claim.

    How it was built

    Read the short interview brief ↗

    Python in desktop VS Code prepares the official data, mortgage scenarios and figures. SQL independently checks every cash and income calculation. Power BI shows the results and the evidence still needed.

    The transaction prices are observed; the buyer profiles and future paths are explicit assumptions. This examines financial barriers to purchase and does not predict prices or approve loans.

    Sources & scope

    Buyer benchmarks: Statistics Canada 46-10-0099-01 (2021–2023), and archived 46-10-0062-01 (2020). City/CSD resident first-time buyers; buyer-based transaction medians with changing property mix. Rates: Bank of Canada funds advanced, fixed five years and over; annual means of twelve monthly averages. Tax sources: municipal and provincial governments. Lending/program rules: CMHC, FCAC, CRA, BC law and OSFI. Source manifest and checksums ↗

    Statistics Canada core housing need, survey years 2018, 2021, 2022 and 2024; ten provinces.     Statistics Canada income, consumption and saving, 2020 to 2025. Purchase rules: CMHC and Finance Canada.
